## USDA mapping and coverage (deliverable 2)
1,080 of 1,549 mappable taxonomy slugs (excludes `category = 'nonfood'`) matched a Foundation or
SR Legacy food: `usda_hint` first, then a scored match of the name + synonyms against every
food's description (a Dice-style recall/precision score, gated so a short query needs every word
present, and a category sanity check so a plant/grain/sweet slug can never accept a match that
mentions meat or fish), then 55 hand-checked entries in `overrides.yaml` for common misses
(margarine-family, spices with verbose USDA descriptions, vinegars, alcohol, a few dairy/produce
substitutes). Weighted by how often each slug appears in the corpus (`ingredients.recipe_count`,
from the existing `corpus_sample.db`): **97.4% of ingredient occurrences mapped** (30,585 /
31,399), well above the 90% bar. Remaining unmapped occurrences are mostly variable spice blends
(`italian_seasoning`, `garam_masala`, `harissa`), a few uncommon peppers, and near-zero-calorie
items (`food_coloring`, `lemon_pepper`, `msg`) — left unmapped by design rather than guessed.

**Design was wrong about: the 2.9% overall fill rate is not a nutrition-mapping problem, it's a
servings problem.** Only 511/3,621 sample recipes (14%) have a parseable `servings` at all
(`ingest/build/curate.py.parse_servings`, out of this slice's scope), and it varies enormously by
source: `recipenlg` (2,100 recipes, the largest source) and `themealdb` (350) carry no yield text
whatsoever -- `servings` is `NULL` for every one of them, so nutrition is NULL by the "servings
must be known" rule regardless of mapping quality. Where servings IS known, the fill rate is much
healthier and tracks mapping/parse quality directly: `bbcgoodfood` 92/179 (51%), `foodwishes`
12/40 (30%), but `foodcom` 0/292 (0%) -- its ingredient lines lose their unit text upstream in the
parser (e.g. "1/4 c sugar" parses to `qty=0.25, unit='piece'`, the parser's fallback when no unit
token is recognized), which this slice's gram conversion correctly refuses to guess at rather
than mistreating a quarter-cup of sugar as a quarter of one piece. Both are upstream, real gaps
(servings parsing and per-source unit recognition), not a nutrition-slice defect; a follow-up
task is queued for the servings side.

Also fixed along the way (both inside `ingest/nutrition/`, no scope creep): (1) USDA's newer
Foundation Foods rows often carry only Atwater conversion-factor energy (ids 2047/2048), not the
plain Energy id (1008) SR Legacy uses -- `usda.py` now falls back 1008 -> 2047 -> 2048 per food;
without it, common ingredients like `ground_beef`, `kidney_beans`, `canned_tomatoes`, `oats` had
every macro but no kcal. (2) most of the taxonomy's ~1,250 non-"top-300" slugs (spices, herbs,
condiments) have no `density_g_per_ml`/`each_g` at all, which would fail nearly every recipe's
80% core-line bar on the herbs/spices alone; `estimate.py` adds a category-typical density
fallback (spice/herb/dairy/etc.) and a unit-typical weight fallback (pinch, sprig, handful, ...)
for exactly these small-quantity, low-calorie units -- a deliberate approximation, documented in
the module docstring, that materially raised coverage without needing taxonomy edits (out of
this slice's scope).
