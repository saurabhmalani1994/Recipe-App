# Brief S13: port the ingredient parser to TypeScript (app)
From the orchestrator. Why: the owner's own recipes (My Recipes, forks) and the later "Import
from URL" are raw text on the phone. Without a parser in the app, they can't be matched, scaled
by unit or added to a grocery list; today they fall into "Check these".
Base: claude/funny-thompson-jcmpox HEAD. Scope: `app/src/parse/` (new), plus wiring in
`app/src/features/myRecipes/` and `app/src/features/plan/recipeLookup.ts`. Read
ingest/parse/parser.py, ingest/parse/units.py and ingest/taxonomy/normalize.py; they are the
reference.
Model: strong. Effort: high.

Deliverable:
1 `app/src/parse/`: a deterministic TS port of the Python parser and name normaliser, producing
  the same output shape. The synonym index comes from the slugs.json the app already ships
  (regenerate it if the normaliser needs more fields).
2 Parity is enforced in code (rule 12). Use a test that runs over ingest/parse/gold.jsonl and
  ingest/fixtures/ingredient_lines_sample.txt, comparing the TS output with a committed
  snapshot of the Python output (generated by a script). Bar: 100% equal on gold, and >= 99.5%
  equal on the 3000-line sample, with every difference listed and explained.
3 My Recipes and forks store parsed items (slug, qty, unit) beside the raw line when they are
  saved (user.db migration). They then flow into scaling, the grocery list and matching. The
  editor shows, under each line, what was understood ("2 cups → cilantro"), and marks lines it
  did not understand.
4 Tests: parity, the save round-trip, and a grocery list built from a My Recipe with real items.
Checks: scripts/check.sh, and e2e with PW_PORT=4310. Report per §6.
